This comparison covers the Citizen NJ0150-56X with its green dial and the Seiko SRPD51 with its blue sunray dial. Both are automatics on stainless-steel bracelets, so the choice comes down to case, crystal, water rating and look.

Citizen doesn’t list a case thickness for the Tsuyosa, so this comparison can’t say which watch sits lower on the wrist. The Seiko’s 13.4 mm is worth checking against your cuffs.

Which one to choose

  • For a smaller case: the Tsuyosa is 2.5 mm narrower. Try a watch of a similar diameter before you order.
  • For more water resistance: the SRPD51 is rated to 100 m against the Tsuyosa’s 50 m. Follow each maker’s usage guidance rather than treating a rating as permission for any water activity.
  • For a harder crystal: the Tsuyosa has sapphire; the SRPD51 has Hardlex. Sapphire is known for scratch resistance, though no crystal makes a watch indestructible.
  • For a sporty look: the SRPD51 has a rotating bezel and a see-through caseback that shows the movement. The Tsuyosa is the plainer design.
  • For wearing it irregularly: both are automatics with roughly two days of power reserve (42 and about 41 hours), so either may need resetting after time off the wrist.

What “5 Sports” means

Seiko’s own product page titles the SRPD51 “Men’s Seiko 5 Sports” and lists its collection as Seiko 5 Sports. That’s the line name, and SRPD51 is the specific reference. Match the reference before you buy, since a listing may show a different 5 Sports model.
