This comparison covers the Orient RA-AA0004E39B Kamasu with its green dial and the Seiko SRPD51 with its blue sunray dial. Both are automatics with day-and-date displays on stainless-steel bracelets, so the choice comes down to water rating, crystal, case and look. Which one to choose

  • For more water resistance: the Kamasu is rated to 200 m against the SRPD51’s 100 m. Follow each maker’s usage guidance rather than treating a rating as permission for any water activity.
  • For a harder crystal: the Kamasu has sapphire; the SRPD51 has Hardlex. Sapphire is known for scratch resistance, though no crystal makes a watch indestructible.
  • For a smaller case: the Kamasu is about 0.7 mm narrower. That’s a small gap, and its 22 mm bracelet still gives it a full-size diver’s presence, so try a watch of similar diameter before you order.
  • For seeing the movement: the SRPD51 has a see-through caseback. Our Kamasu guide doesn’t record one.
  • For wearing it irregularly: both are automatics with roughly two days of power reserve (40 and about 41 hours), and both can be hand-wound, so either may need winding and resetting after time off the wrist.

Who each suits

The Kamasu suits someone who wants a diver’s water rating and sapphire in an automatic, and doesn’t mind a full-size look. The SRPD51 suits someone who likes the Seiko 5 Sports styling, with its rotating bezel and visible movement, and wants to check a 13.4 mm case against their cuffs. Neither comparison is a test of real-world durability or comfort.

What “5 Sports” means

Seiko’s own product page titles the SRPD51 “Men’s Seiko 5 Sports” and lists its collection as Seiko 5 Sports. That’s the line name, and SRPD51 is the specific reference. Match the reference before you buy, since a listing may show a different 5 Sports model. The same applies to the Kamasu: confirm the RA-AA0004E39B reference and green dial.
