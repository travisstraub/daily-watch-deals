Compare the space on your wrist

The A168W is the widest at 36.3 mm, the F-91W is 35.2 mm and the A158WA is the narrowest at 33.2 mm. The A158WA is also the thinnest (8.2 mm against 8.5 mm and 9.6 mm) and the shortest case (36.8 mm). The weight gap comes mostly from the band: the F-91W weighs 21 g, while the two steel-bracelet models weigh 46 g and 50 g. Compare these numbers with a watch you already wear, including its length. They help you judge size, but can’t guarantee comfort. A bracelet may also need sizing.

Which one to choose

  • For the lightest watch: the F-91W at 21 g. It’s the only resin-strap model of the three, and the only one with a resin case.
  • For the slimmest metal watch: the A158WA at 8.2 mm thick and 33.2 mm wide, the narrowest of the three. Its LED light is weaker than an electro-luminescent backlight.
  • For the strongest backlight: the A168W has an electro-luminescent backlight. It’s also the widest and thickest, at 36.3 × 9.6 mm.
  • For a steel look: the A168W and A158WA both come on steel bracelets. The A158WA is the smaller of the two; the A168W has the chrome-plated case.
  • For the basics: all three have a daily alarm, a stopwatch and about seven years of battery life.

All three carry only the basic water-resistance rating and resin glass, so none is a swimming watch and all call for care around scratches. Follow Casio’s usage guidance rather than treating a rating as permission for any water activity.
